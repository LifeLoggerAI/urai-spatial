# Founder and Event Field Guide

## The simplest accurate explanation

> URAI is building a spatial operating system for a person's life. Instead of reducing memory, relationships, goals, and identity to folders, feeds, and dashboards, it turns them into a private world a person can navigate, reflect on, and control.

Do not begin with version numbers, architecture, patent counts, autonomous agents, or the full roadmap. Begin with the human problem.

## Natural opening at an event

> Hey, I'm Adam. I'm building URAI. We are creating a different way for people to interact with their own memories, relationships, goals, and life story. Instead of putting everything into another dashboard, URAI turns it into a spatial world you can move through. What are you working on?

After they answer:

> That is interesting. The reason I started URAI is that our lives are spread across photos, messages, notes, calendars, and apps, but none of those systems helps us see the whole picture. We are building a private life map where memories can become places, relationships can become constellations, and goals can become destinations. I have a short demo if you want to see it.

## 15-second version

> URAI turns your memories, relationships, goals, and life story into a private spatial world you can navigate, instead of another feed or dashboard.

## 30-second version

> Most technology stores pieces of your life but never helps you understand the whole thing. URAI is building a spatial operating system where memories become navigable experiences, relationships form constellations, goals become destinations, and the user controls what the system can access, remember, reflect, or export. We have a substantial web experience now and are working toward a claim-safe public beta.

## Two-minute founder version

> Today, a person's life is fragmented across photos, messages, documents, calendars, health systems, social platforms, and private memories. Existing software organizes files or generates answers, but it does not give a person a coherent way to see and navigate their own life.
>
> URAI is building a spatial operating system for memory, identity, reflection, focus, and personal direction. The core experience moves through Home, Ground, Life Map, Focus, Replay, Mirror, Passport, and Status. A memory can exist as a place or artifact. A relationship can exist as a constellation. A goal can exist as a destination. Replay can reconstruct a meaningful story from source-backed material. Passport is the permission layer that keeps the person in control.
>
> What makes this different is that the interface is not just a chatbot or dashboard. It is an explorable world organized around the person. We already have a substantial fallback/demo spatial web experience, route structure, runtime architecture, privacy boundaries, and release infrastructure. We are now closing the final public-beta gaps: exact deployment proof, route parity, production assets, authenticated data isolation, and end-to-end evidence.
>
> We are looking for early users, design partners, technical and clinical-safety advisers where appropriate, and investors who understand that personal AI will need a human-owned interface, not just a better chat window.

## What to say when someone says, "That sounds crazy"

> It is a big idea, but the first step is concrete. We are not asking someone to believe the entire future at once. We show one journey: enter the world, select a memory in the Life Map, move into Focus, and see it become a Replay. The larger vision becomes understandable once people experience that transition.

## What to say when asked, "Is it live?"

> A substantial V1 web experience is reachable and demonstrable. We are still finishing production certification, exact deployment receipts, final assets, and deeper authenticated data integration, so I describe it as a working fallback/demo experience rather than a finished production platform.

## What to say when asked, "Are you raising?"

> We are building relationships with investors now while we close the public-beta evidence gates. The right conversation is about funding the move from a substantial working experience into a secure, certified beta with real users, final assets, and measurable retention.

## What to say to a potential customer or pilot partner

> I am not asking you to buy a finished enterprise platform today. I am looking for a small number of design partners who will help us test whether this way of organizing memory, identity, and direction creates real value. The first step would be a controlled demo and a conversation about one use case.

## One clear ask per person

Use only one:

- "Would you be open to a 20-minute follow-up demo?"
- "Who is the one person here you think should see this?"
- "Would your organization consider a small design-partner pilot?"
- "What proof would you need before considering an investment?"
- "Would you test the beta and give structured feedback?"

## Questions to ask investors

- What milestone would make this investable to you?
- Do you see this first as consumer, caregiver, creator, veteran, education, or enterprise infrastructure?
- Which part feels most defensible: spatial interface, permission model, memory graph, replay, or distribution?
- What would you need to see around retention and trust?
- Who else should understand this before we raise formally?

## Questions to ask founders and operators

- Where does important personal or institutional context get lost in your current workflow?
- What information do people repeatedly reconstruct by hand?
- Where would a spatial or narrative interface be more useful than a dashboard?
- What privacy concern would stop adoption immediately?
- Would you rather test one narrow use case or the complete journey?

## Conversation close

> I do not want to overload you with the whole roadmap. Let me send you a one-page overview and the short demo link. Then we can have a focused conversation about the part that is relevant to you.

## Follow-up message

> Great meeting you tonight. I appreciated your perspective on [specific topic]. URAI is the spatial life-operating-system project I showed you: a private way to navigate memories, relationships, goals, reflection, and personal evidence. Here is the short overview and demo. The most useful next step would be a 20-minute conversation about [pilot / investment milestone / introduction].

## Event preparation checklist

- Use a charged phone and laptop.
- Preload the exact demo route.
- Use sample data only.
- Turn off notifications.
- Carry a QR code to one stable landing page.
- Keep the first demo under 60 seconds.
- Know the current claim boundary before arriving.
- Seek three meaningful follow-ups, not a stack of random contacts.
- Write one sentence about each person immediately after the conversation.
- Send follow-ups within 24 hours.
